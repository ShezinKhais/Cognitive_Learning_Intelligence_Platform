# Optional teaching pilot (#259)

Use this plan if the team and course lead agree to run a small, voluntary
classroom trial. The aim is to learn whether C.L.I.P. helps lecturers decide
what to explain again and helps students find the right material to revise.

## Run one lesson

1. Pick one topic, one set of checkpoint questions and one revision task.
   Agree in advance what a useful intervention and a successful revision
   task look like. Record the C.L.I.P. release, client and lesson format.
2. Explain the trial and get consent before collecting feedback. Participation
   is voluntary and has no effect on grades. Leave camera, microphone and
   live transcription off for the pilot.
3. After a checkpoint, ask the lecturer whether the results changed what
   they explained next. Record the action and a brief reason, or record
   that there was no change.
4. Ask students to use the tutor after the lesson to find a cited source
   for one topic. Record whether they found the correct source and could
   explain the key idea in their own words. Count partial and incomplete
   tasks separately, and ask what made the task easier or harder.
5. Summarize results as counts, such as tasks completed out of tasks
   attempted. Report the size of the group, missing answers and any factors
   that could have affected the outcome. A single small trial can surface
   friction and useful examples. It cannot establish a learning improvement.

## Record the result
